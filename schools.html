Classrooms

Schools

One lesson. Two veterans. Then the children ask. Forms, activity guides, and — as they are written — the accounts the veterans tell in the room.

How the hour works

  • Length One hour
  • Speakers Two veterans
  • Stories Fifteen minutes each
  • Then Thirty minutes of questions and answers with the children

No parade and no politics lesson. School staff stay in the room. Speakers are asked to keep language and detail suitable for the year group. A child who does not take part is given an alternative activity by the school.

The first Oxfordshire visits are being booked now. Confirmed schools and dates go on the events page.

Parent consent form

One form per child. The school sends it home, keeps the signed copy, and may put it on its own letterhead.

Activity guides

A teacher pack for the visit: how to run the half-hour of questions, and short tasks for Key Stages 1, 2 and 3. Print the pack, or use one stage only.

Teacher notes

Before, during and after. Questions that work. Questions to steer away from. Staff stay in the room.

Key Stage 1

Years 1–2. Listen, remember one thing, draw it, write a short caption, make a class thank-you.

Key Stage 2

Years 3–6. Prepare two questions. Who / what / where. An 80–120 word recount or a letter that names one detail.

Key Stage 3

Years 7–9. Compare the two accounts. A short written piece or a five-minute pair presentation. Civic, not party-political.
